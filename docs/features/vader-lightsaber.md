# Feature: Darth Vader’s Lightsaber
Status: regenerating durability deployed and server-verified; rendered client playtest pending
Branch: bilal/vader-lightsaber (original throw); bilal/tusken-mobs (durability follow-up)

## Player experience
`/mmc give vader` issues one shield-based Darth Vader lightsaber; it also appears in Weapons in the admin browser. The hilt appears in GUI/ground contexts, the red blade when held, and the original JustTimm blocking pose on use. Holding/deselecting plays legacy ignition sounds. Item lore follows the accepted badge/blank-line/description/yellow-keybind standard.

## Rules
Main-hand attack uses native attack attributes and charge. Right-click blocks; incoming front-facing E11 bolts and arrows reflect toward the defender’s aim. No timed parry. Side/rear bolts do not reflect. Each block/reflection spends charge directly proportional to incoming damage: one damage point costs five guard points (5% with the default 100-point capacity), shown by the native item durability bar instead of a heat action-bar message. Charge repairs at 0.5 points per tick (10% per second) after 100 quiet ticks (5 seconds) without charge use or positive damage dealt/taken. Zero charge clears use, immediately returns an airborne saber, and stuns blocking, reflection, attacking, swinging, mining and throwing for at least 80 ticks and while charge remains empty. With the five-second quiet delay, actions resume only after charge starts recovering. There is no instant refill. Native shield cooldown feedback follows the stun, and switching saber items cannot bypass it. Charge remains player-owned, so switching sabers or native repair cannot reset it. Every carried saber displays the same charge. Native saber wear is cancelled and the item cannot break from charge use; ordinary tools retain native wear and repair. The guard continues recovering while stowed or offline in the running server; a server restart resets this session-owned state. Offhand saber use is denied.

Sneak + attack launches a returning saber and spends 75% of full charge gradually over 20 ticks (one second): a full saber reaches 25%. Throw activation requires at least the full 75% cost, including any already reserved drain. Exactly 75% can launch and empties into the stun; lower charge is denied without creating a flight. Drain completes even after an early catch/recall, then uses the same idle delay and repair speed as reflection. The flight item, displayed item and saved airborne item all receive the current durability; catch restores that charge with the original other metadata. The hand and original inventory slot are empty during flight. The flight reserves the exact item, including its metadata; player persistent data stores its serialized item and original slot so a saved airborne state can recover on join. Catch, slot change, teleport, logout and shutdown return that item once. Inventory transfers/drops/swaps and pickups are blocked during flight. If another plugin fills the slot, the saber returns elsewhere in inventory, or becomes an owner-bound drop if inventory is full. Normal death adds the airborne saber to the death drops; keep-inventory death returns it to inventory. Login also normalizes legacy hilt presentation from earlier builds.

The throw now follows one analytic oval with a continuous circulation direction. The user rejected the previous crossing path as a figure-eight and requested a rounder/circular sweep. A modest baseline width gives the ends room to curve while keeping the oval compact relative to its reach. An assisted throw rotates the complete oval so the outward leg intersects the chosen mob; it does not pull a waypoint across the return leg. Vertical variation lifts the recovery, and smooth target/hand correction preserves continuous motion. Per-launch distance and curvature tables retain gradual braking through bends and acceleration afterward.

The accepted 18-block reach, spin coefficient 38, angle-driven tilt and substantial per-throw variation remain. Each launch samples width, rise, recovery height, bank strength, spin and tempo; 30% mirror the usual side at full variation. Tilt follows travel slope and signed turning angle, with damped quaternion interpolation capped at six degrees per tick for the spin plane. Spin remains independent of tilt. The banking approach was informed by [Craig Reynolds' steering paper](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/steer/gdc99/); the saber's path and tuning are our implementation.

The initial aim selects one visible, damageable mob nearest the crosshair inside an 18-degree half-cone, excluding the owner's tame pet. Tracking corrects at most 0.15 blocks per tick within 1.5 blocks of its original position, then drops without retargeting if obstructed, dead or too far away. Tracking also stops on impact or after the attacking crossing, so the saber does not chase a knocked-back mob. Close targets shorten the loop. Without a target it follows an oval oriented to release aim. Vertical aim remains supported.

Render and collision share a centered, rotating two-block blade pose derived from the pinned upstream fixed-context model. Translation and rotation are subdivided by maximum tip travel (0.12 blocks); hit tests cover the complete blade segment plus the configured thickness. A pass can hit multiple nearby victims. Both legs damage on each separate pass; spinning while the same victim remains within the blade's reach counts as one sustained contact. Leaving that reach and crossing again permits another hit. Each contact temporarily bypasses the previous native hurt window while preserving its prior state and Bukkit damage cancellation. Accepted hits play the existing impact sound and a short critical/spark burst; native hurt flash and death effects remain. Paper's cancellable knockback event redirects the native horizontal impulse along the blade's travel, preserving resistance and vertical response. Cancelled damage produces no custom hit effect.

The blade and its travel check blocks, protected areas and loaded chunks before damage. Permission samples are at most 0.25 blocks apart, with block-coordinate results shared within one move. A wall or denied endpoint triggers direct recall; a blocked recall restores the existing item without crossing the obstruction. No terrain edits or chunk generation are requested. Flight lifetime remains bounded to 100 ticks.

Combat is denied in SpawnWorld and the survival landing, including throw starts, flight endpoints, victims and reflected-shot destinations. Bukkit damage events preserve vendor/future clan cancellation; clan-specific behavior remains deferred. WeaponMechanics retains responsibility for E11 damage and final hit processing.

## Smallest playable version
One Vader saber, one E11 interaction, one regenerating durability meter and a returning throw. Tuning is provisional, not final gear balance. Crafting and Force powers remain future work. The full named saber catalog is now implemented; see lightsaber-catalog.md.

## Ownership and integrations
MandoMC owns catalog identity `vader` / `mandomc:item_id`, item attributes, shared charge and flight lifecycle. WeaponMechanics 4.3.1 owns projectile simulation/damage. Its immutable shooter field requires constructing a new reflected projectile, not simply changing the old bolt’s direction. The public API adapter preserves weapon configuration/speed and bounds reflection chains; the defender owns the new bolt.

JustTimm owns the artwork. His terms permit server use but prohibit rehosting extracted assets without permission. The original version 3.2.2 ZIP is downloaded by clients from its official Modrinth CDN on player join, with a deduplicated pack-loaded fallback. Its SHA-1 and URL are pinned in sabers.yml and dependencies.json. The original implementation referenced three Vader model entry points; the full named catalog now references 231 upstream entry points (see lightsaber-catalog.md). The upstream pack has no font/shader overrides; its named-item shield selection does not control our explicitly assigned item_model. Credits and thanks are included in resource-pack/src/credits.txt and the pack README.

Legacy improvements: directional reflection instead of omnidirectional protection; correct reflected-shot owner; player-owned charge displayed through durability; bounded shared scheduler instead of a task per throw; one reserved item with persisted ownership and exact return; safe-zone checks on each damage path. Content is config, vendor APIs stay in integrations, and no parallel item registry is introduced.

## Parameters
`items/definitions/sabers.yml` owns name, lore, model, rarity, native attack-damage (10.5) and attack-speed (1.0). `combat/sabers.yml` owns behavior: throw damage 8 per pass, cooldown 40 ticks, range 18 blocks, nominal outward speed 1, return cruise/recall cap 1.5, blade hit thickness 0.22 blocks, arc width coefficient 1.8, launch height coefficient 0.65, return height coefficient 2.4, bank limit coefficient 25 degrees, spin coefficient 38 degrees/tick (previously 42), target half-angle 18 degrees, tracking correction limit 1.5 blocks and variation 1.0. Variation is configurable from 0 (fixed style) to 1 (full range); the existing schema receives additive defaults.

At full variation, width scales 0.55–1.25×, launch rise 0.15–1.8×, recovery height 0.55–1.45×, bank limit 0.45–1.5× and spin 28.5–53.2 degrees/tick. Shape coefficients control the oval span and vertical variation, not literal maximum offsets; width/height shrink at close range. The oval has no crossing or separately stitched turn segment, and curvature-aware timing spreads tight turns over multiple ticks. Its half-width is 0.06 × reach + 0.85 × sampled width, capped to 0.3 × reach; the complete three-dimensional curve is scaled to remain within reach. Tempo varies 0.88–1.15×. Per-launch distance and curvature tables drive progress, with gradual speed changes through bends and at the catch; the old fixed-duration curve clock and return catch-up clamp are removed. Return speed is a nominal cruise divided by tempo, while emergency recall uses it as a strict cap. Flight lifetime remains 100 ticks. The new optional `throw-spin-degrees` key defaults to 38 for existing configs; the installed local range was explicitly updated to 18.

Guard capacity 100, cost five guard points per incoming damage point, repair 0.5/tick after 100 quiet ticks, exhaustion lockout at least 80 ticks and while empty; reflection half-angle 80 degrees and chain limit 4. `language/messages.yml` owns feedback. Item content uses `/mmc items reload`; behavior and dependency pins require restart. Existing inventory melee attributes are not automatically rebalanced. Throw charge adds `throw-cost-percent` (0–100, default 75) and `throw-drain-ticks` (1–100, default 20), with additive defaults for existing schema-version 1 configs. `guard-damage-multiplier` (0.01–100, default 5) scales blocked/reflected damage only; throw cost stays 75%. Set existing installs’ `guard-recovery-delay-ticks` to 100 for the five-second quiet window. The fixed `guard-cost-per-hit` key is retired and ignored in existing configs. Other guard tuning keys keep their names for compatibility. WeaponMechanics reflection runs at its damage event after Tusken level scaling; the vendor computes armor, hit location, movement, range and critical modifiers with only its shield-negation modifier excluded. Native blocks use the blocked damage amount, and arrows reflect after native damage calculation. Cancelled hits spend no charge. The new `sabers.tired-use`, `tired-attack`, `tired-block` and `tired-throw` messages fall back to bundled defaults and say “You are too tired to …”. Old heat/overheated/depleted/throw-cooldown translations are unused. Existing carried sabers migrate to the charge display automatically on the next tick. No artwork or resource-pack release is needed.

Adding item families now installs missing bundled rarity/category badge keys without overwriting operator choices. The existing robes remain unchanged.

## Durability follow-up
The user requested durability-based reflection feedback and a large, fast gradual throw drain now that ranged Tuskens exist. Native durability is the charge display, not a second wear system. The accepted throw path and empty-hand ownership remain unchanged. After a full-charge throw finishes draining, another throw becomes available after about 10 quiet seconds (5-second delay plus 5 seconds to recover from 25% to 75%). A full refill takes about 12.5 quiet seconds after the drain completes. Rendered client feel and reflection balance still need playtesting.
